# Castle Control - Part 2: UI

Snapshot of the Claude Doc tab "Part 2: UI", 2026-10-25. Revised for the operator's verdict of 2026-10-26: two-layer chambers and custom design.

## Summary

The product is called **Castle Control**, a working name that is not a cleared trademark. Its UI comes from two contest rounds that the owner decided on 2026-10-25:

- **Round 1.** Two seats built six brands, each with a landing page and all four module pages, on illustrative Overlord-sandbox data: Castle Control, Multiball (twice), Proof, Shepherd and Overprint. The owner chose **Overprint** (B/3) as "the best combination of style, clarity", noted that it fits Shadowrunr well, and rejected its brand.
- **Final round.** Two seats each rebranded Overprint's craft. Overprint's typography, paper background, ink-and-halftone art and app density were kept. One seat made Castle Control and the other made Shepherd. **Castle Control won.**

The app keeps Overprint's paper, inks, type and panel rules with Castle Control's names, mark and voice. The operator's 2026-10-26 verdict replaces the stacked app layouts with two-layer chambers. The landing page is a new design: the keep in cross-section, printed in spot inks.

The winning prototype runs from disk on illustrative data. It is evidence for the port, not a code base to copy; the port contract at the end of this file says how it moves into the real app.

## Brand

**The idea.** A streamer should feel sovereign and safe. The channel is a keep, chat is the crowd at the gate, and the four tools are chambers the streamer commands. The brand is *printed, not rendered*: a gig-poster print in three heraldic spot inks plus a key colour, on warm paper.

- **Tagline:** "Your channel is your keep."
- **Mark:** the keep-bubble, a chat bubble with a battlement top, printed in gules with a gate lit in or. One shared component draws it everywhere.
- **Voice:** courteous and brief. Every castle word sits beside its plain function, so a streamer never has to learn the metaphor to use the tool.

| Chamber | Module id | Ink (tincture) | Hex | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lot Chamber | `lottery` | Gules (red) | `#E4472E` | Wax seals and banners |
| Gatehouse | `moderator` | Azure (blue) | `#3F86DC` | The watch, water, the moat |
| Beacons | `visuals` | Or (gold) | `#F2B233` | Fire and gilt |

Inks overprint with a multiply blend, so two overlapping inks make a third colour, as on a real press. Text uses darker companions of each ink (`--azure-text` `#1D5BAA`, `--gules-text` `#B3321B`, `--or-text` `#7A5800`) for contrast on paper.

## Type, paper and tokens

The prototype declares every token once, in `tokens.css`. The port extracts these values unchanged: names may change, pixels may not.

| Role | Token | Stack in the prototype |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display (titles, chamber names) | `--font-display` | Bahnschrift, Arial Narrow, Roboto Condensed, Helvetica Neue, Impact |
| Serif (taglines, italic asides) | `--font-serif` | Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Palatino, Georgia |
| UI text | `--font-ui` | Segoe UI Variable Text, Segoe UI, system-ui, -apple-system, Helvetica Neue, Arial |
| Live figures (tabular) | `--font-num` | ui-monospace, Cascadia Mono, SF Mono, Consolas, Menlo |

- **Paper:** `--paper` `#F4F0E6`, with a grain texture. `--paper-2` `#EAE4D4`, card `#FBF9F3`, rules `#D9D2BE`, key ink `#16181D`.
- **Dark stock:** a full theme under `[data-theme="stock"]`. It sets paper `#121317` and text `#F2EEE3`, lightens the ink-text companions, and switches the blend to screen. The landing and the app share the toggle, and it exists because a four-hour stream needs it.
- **Signals:** ok `#0A9A5E`, warn `#E8A800`, danger `#CC2149`, info `#1D6FC4`. Their dark-stock values are lighter.
- **Shape and motion:** radii of 3, 6 and 12 px; one easing curve, `cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)`.

**Type across platforms.** The stacks lean on Windows faces: Bahnschrift and Segoe UI Variable. To keep the look on macOS and Linux, the app bundles open-licence fallbacks with close metrics after the system faces. The Windows look is unchanged. Chosen by the plan, not by the owner.

## The app shell

Every chamber page shares one frame, built for 1440x900 with OBS open beside it, and still usable at 1280x800.

- **The rampart (masthead), always visible:**
  - the ON AIR stamp with viewers and uptime;
  - Twitch and OBS connection marks;
  - the Gatehouse chip, showing "Armed n | Watching n", or "Bridge up" when Shield Mode is on;
  - a sample-data or simulated label whenever data is not live;
  - settings, the theme toggle and the Steward button.
- **The chambers column:** the four chambers, each with its live figure, such as "3 draws · 87 lots" or "3 held · 2 watching". Below them sit the whole-keep and brand-sheet links, and a "the hall now" footer with viewers, chatters, tier and moat.
- **The page header:** a breadcrumb ("Castle Control › Chamber 2 of 4 › Gatehouse"), the chamber title in display type with its ink ghost, a one-line plain description, and page-level actions.
- **Focus views:** numbered sections keep their panel rules inside separate nested pages. Live figures use tabular numerals, and body text is at least 13 px.
- **Nested pages:** each adjustment opens as its own focus view with a breadcrumb and Back. The writ (proof bundle) and the drill (replay) also open as separate pages.
- **Keymap:** keys 1-4 switch chambers and `/` is search. Tab and Enter open focus views; Escape or Back returns. The port keeps one keymap table with a conflict check. Shortcuts do not intercept typing in inputs.

Two invariants hold on every page: the streamer can tell whether they are live, and what the Gatehouse is doing (armed or watching).

## Two-layer chambers

The operator's verdict of 2026-10-26 replaces the hero band and dense panel stack with two layers. All four chambers use this model.

**Layer 1: the stage.** One dominant abstract graphical stage fills a shared stage frame. The frame is a single component across all four chambers. Its content changes by purpose: the roll or urn, the gate and room, the beacon fires, or the map. Beside it sit at most three headline figures and at most two primary actions. The rampart keeps live status and the Gatehouse state visible.

A row of entry points has one labelled link per nested view listed below. It can wrap at smaller sizes. Each link opens its view; it does not expand a panel below the stage. Restyle and Custom design remain available as secondary artifact controls and do not add primary actions.

**Layer 2: focused adjustments.** Every current numbered panel becomes a standalone focus view opened from the stage. Tables, forms, logs and detailed controls live here. Views are never stacked on one page or opened as drawers. Each has a breadcrumb, such as `Castle Control > Lot Chamber > 1.3 Next draw setup`, and Back.

Every entry point is keyboard reachable. Tab reaches it and Enter opens it. Escape or Back returns to the stage and restores focus to the entry point. The same return rule applies to the writ and drill. Keys 1-4 switch chambers and `/` is search through the shared keymap.

## Chamber pages

Each chamber has one stage and the following standalone focus views. Numbers name views, not a vertical page layout. Gatehouse numbers follow today's desktop panels; Beacons includes its existing layers view and Map Room keeps its prototype panels.

### Lot Chamber (lottery manager)

**Layer 1.** The roll or urn dominates the frame. Numbered tickets and a cohort-coloured dot field show intake. The pool description and open, paused or sealed state label the image. The three headline figures are entries, winner count and cooldown exclusions. The two primary actions are Seal the roll and Pause intake. Once sealed, Cast the lots replaces Seal the roll when the public round is verified. A sealed roll cannot resume intake or change winners.

**Layer 2 entry points:**

- **1.1 Draws you have:** saved draws, with No prize, Prize and Paid weight blocked tags.
- **1.2 The roll:** entries, cohorts and lookup by handle.
- **1.3 Next draw setup:** name, content (question, idea, prize or seat), pool, keyword, restrictions, a free !join path with equal odds, weights, winners, cooldown and cohort cap.
- **1.4 Fairness you can verify:** seal, announced public round, round arrival and cast, with a test-mode note.
- **1.5 On stream:** draw-shape presets, preview, Restyle and Custom design.
- **1.6 Past draws:** completed runs, winners and links to each proof bundle.
- **The writ:** the selected run's proof bundle, download and independent recompute. It also opens from 1.6 and returns to its caller with Back.

### Gatehouse (AI moderator)

**Layer 1.** The gate and room show the crowd passing, waiting or barred. Garrison orders and the room tier label the image. The three headline figures are held messages, pending bans and chat delay in seconds. The sole primary action toggles Shield Mode: Raise the drawbridge or Lower the drawbridge. Rules and human review stay in their focus views. The rampart still shows Armed and Watching counts.

**Layer 2 entry points:**

- **2.0 The room:** the S/M/L/XL tier ladder, hysteresis, garrison orders and timed leases.
- **2.1 The live room:** chat with Pass, Hold or Bar, search, filters and Pause live.
- **2.2 The Herald's chart:** opinion directions over 15 minutes, text and emote filters, shares and 3-minute deltas. It describes the room and cannot act on anyone.
- **2.3 The rules:** rule, match, action and Armed/Watching controls. Shadow is the default.
- **2.4 At the portcullis:** held messages with Pass and Strike, plus the chat-delay control.
- **2.5 Pending bans:** original message, rule, Ban and Dismiss. Human approval remains the default.
- **2.6 The gate roll:** the decision ledger with reasons, Undo and Petition.
- **2.7 The drill:** recording selection, replay and rule-set diff in a dedicated focus view. Results stay labelled simulated and never write to the live ledger.

### Beacons (contribution visuals)

**Layer 1.** Fires show the selected visual's current intensity in the shared frame. Offering altar, gilded camera frame and hype border are selectable presets. The three headline figures are blaze percentage, current contribution total and decay time. The two primary actions are Test fire: 50-sub bomb and Copy browser-source URL. Simulated bursts and fixture URLs stay visibly labelled.

**Layer 2 entry points:**

- **3.1 What is feeding the beacons:** contribution event feed, input sources and dedupe status.
- **3.2 Kindling curve:** input-to-blaze curve, current point, decay and gift-sub weighting.
- **3.3 Test fire:** preview, intensity slider, simulated bursts and source URL details.
- **3.4 Layers of the selected beacon:** image, shape, text and particle layers, visual presets, Restyle and Custom design. Money changes visuals only.

### Map Room (Extension studio)

**Layer 1.** The map dominates the frame. The selected template supplies its board, house banners and fog. The three headline figures are time until turn end, participating viewers and current turn number. The sole primary action is Run a test turn. Inspection and operator adjustments open focus views.

**Layer 2 entry points:**

- **4.1 Pieces:** board, turn clock, move menu, side tallies and standing-meter components, with selected-piece inspection.
- **4.2 Orders:** human, AI or AI proposes/human approves; viewer actions, cadence and sealing.
- **4.3 Templates:** hex map, choice board, viewer card, draw and contribution meter as presets, with Restyle and Custom design.
- **4.4 Spyglass:** desktop and mobile viewer previews, side filters and fog.
- **4.5 Test status:** sand table, muster for allowlisted accounts, and proclaimed status. Showing a stage does not grant approval to publish.

## Presets, restyle and custom design

Every visual artifact is a preset the streamer can change. This includes draw shapes, beacon visuals, `overlord-urn`, Map Room templates and overlays. Each artifact always offers **Restyle** to choose among presets and **Custom design** to request a new style or interface. These controls sit beside the artifact and in its design focus view.

Custom design uses the multi-provider LLM engine from decision D10. Claude Code CLI drives development. Codex CLI and the other configured providers use the same engine boundary. The streamer describes a style or asks the LLM to code the artifact's interface on demand. No new provider is introduced by this UI plan.

Generated output is either renderer scene/style parameters as data or code in an isolated frame with no network. The frame has no app bridge, credentials, loopback RPC access or authority to change configuration. Code stays outside the app shell; no eval runs there and the existing no-eval scan stays green. Bundled frame code cannot fetch remote scripts or resources. Extension exports remain data-only and obey the reviewed shell's no-eval rule.

The current look and generated preview appear side by side with a clear simulated or draft label. Schema validation happens at one boundary before preview. A failure shows an error and leaves the current look intact. **Seal it** applies the previewed revision; **Tear it up** discards it. A stale proposal must be previewed again against the current revision. Neither generation nor preview changes live output, draw results, moderation rules or viewer permissions.

The full flow is offline-testable against the fake CLI. Simulated fixtures cover a data result, an isolated code result, malformed output, failure, a stale revision, sealing and tearing up. No cloud call, real chat or credential is needed for this test.

### Proposed request/result shapes

All identifiers in this subsection are **PROPOSED: to land in the contract first**. They are design notes, not registered methods or approved wire types. A separate `cc-types` and contract delivery must define their schemas before any caller uses them. This revision adds nothing to Part 3's identifier table.

| Proposed identifier | Request or result shape |
| --- | --- |
| `visual.design.generate` | Request `VisualDesignRequest`; result `VisualDesignProposal`. Generates a draft only. |
| `VisualDesignRequest` | `{ module, artifact_id, base_revision, prompt, output_kind }`. `module` is `lots`, `gatehouse`, `beacons` or `maproom`. IDs and revisions are nonblank strings. `prompt` is nonblank text. `output_kind` is `renderer_data` or `isolated_code`. |
| `VisualDesignProposal` | `{ proposal_id, artifact_id, base_revision, preview_revision, output, simulated }`. IDs and revisions are strings; `simulated` is boolean. `output` is the discriminated result below. |
| `VisualDesignOutput` | Either `{ kind: "renderer_data", scene, style }`, using renderer-owned schemas for scene and style parameters; or `{ kind: "isolated_code", html, css, javascript }`, using strings for a local frame bundle. No output can carry app-shell code or network privileges. |
| `visual.design.seal` | Request `{ proposal_id, base_revision, preview_revision }`; result `{ artifact_id, applied_revision }`. Applies exactly the previewed proposal only after a human seal and a current-revision check. |
| `visual.design.discard` | Request `{ proposal_id }`; result `{ proposal_id, discarded: true }`. Tears up the draft without changing the artifact. |

Proposed records reject unknown fields. Absent optional keys are omitted, never null. Errors use the existing error envelope. The contract delivery must specify renderer schemas, payload limits and frame validation before implementation.

Build the Lot Chamber prototype first behind a fixture route with simulated data. Demonstrate the stage, every focus view, keyboard return and the restyle/custom-design preview flow there. The other three chambers follow only after the operator approves the Lot Chamber pattern. At rollout, the single shared stage frame moves into `packages/ui`; each chamber supplies its own content. This plan change does not implement that rollout.

## The landing

The landing is the project's website, not a screen inside the app. It ships as a static site from the same repo and shares the tokens package.

- **Hero:**
  - a fixed-proportion 1600x1000 sheet, scaled from 1280x800 to 2560x1440;
  - the keep in cross-section: gatehouse, cutaway hall and map room, beacon tower and watch tower, over a moat;
  - the stone shaded in halftone, with crop and registration marks at the corners;
  - a stamped "Castle Control" wordmark and the tagline card.
- **Intro.** Five plates arrive out of register and snap into it: stone, beacon fire, the Lot Chamber, the Gatehouse, then the map. Then the drawbridge drops and control passes to the visitor. It takes under three seconds and can be skipped. Reduced motion gets a calm version.
- **Choosing.** Hovering a chamber ghosts the other plates and prints its name in the sky, in its own ink. Clicking opens a full-bleed print of that chamber, such as the gate roll beside the portcullis, the moat gauge and the Herald's chart. A feature list sits one level deeper, with Back at every level.
- **The banner bar.** A bar always on screen with all four chambers and Open the app.
- **Mobile (390x844).** A stacked wordmark, four chamber strips with their art, and the banner bar.
- **Honesty labels.** Stylised illustration, drawn rather than captured; sample data from a fictional channel; a working name; not affiliated with Twitch.

## Port contract

**Where the prototype lives.** The winning prototype is kept at the repo root in [`prototype/`](../../prototype/). Open `prototype/index.html` from disk; it loads its own fallback copy of the sample data. It holds:

- `index.html` with `landing.css` and `landing.js` (the landing);
- `lottery.html`, `moderator.html`, `visuals.html` and `studio.html`, each with an `app-*.js` file;
- `app.css` and `app.js` (the shared shell);
- `tokens.css`, `mark.js` and `brand.html`;
- `NOTES.md`, the seat's own account of the design and of what changed in the final round.

WP 0.1 moved it into `prototype/` as a read-only reference. It was built in the contest arena at `.contest/arena/toolkit-landing-r2/`, which is not version-controlled. Its stacked panels document the original design. The two-layer model above governs the redesign.

**Known defects to fix in the port, not to carry:**

- At desktop sizes the landing wordmark's L feet are hidden, so it reads "CASTIE CONTROI". It renders correctly at 390 px.
- At 1280 px the Beacons and Map Room pages wrap a column below, so they run tall.
- The Lot Chamber hero can ellipsise a long draw name.
- Input styles and section subcomponents drifted from the prototype in the app port. Fix them inside the redesign and compare their focus, disabled and validation states. The new navigation does not excuse visual drift.

**Exit for the UI port.** All four must hold at once (from the contest's port-quality contract):

1. **Fidelity.** Every page is compared with the prototype at 1440x900 and 1280x800, with zero deviations on the named roles: paper, inks, type, panel rules and the rampart. The approved stage and focus-view layout replaces the panel stack. Compare retained input styles and section subcomponents in their new views. Every named interaction has been driven, including Tab/Enter entry, Escape/Back return, Restyle, Custom design, Seal it and Tear it up.
2. **Gates at zero, each proven red once.** This covers:
   - import boundaries and cycles;
   - no `any` and no `as unknown as`;
   - `JSON.parse` only at the contract door;
   - a 400-line file cap;
   - token drift, and no raw colours or px values in component styles;
   - a type floor of 13 px.
3. **Budgets.** Every list that can outgrow one screen is virtualised and proven at ten times the sample's volume, for example 4,200 chat lines instead of 420. Each route has a first-load bundle budget, measured by byte count.
4. **Prototype code stays out of the product.** The port extracts the prototype's design; it does not copy its code. Hand-written CSS moves as co-located stylesheets over generated token variables, not as approximate utility classes.
