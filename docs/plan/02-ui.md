# Castle Control — Part 2: UI

Snapshot of the Claude Doc tab "Part 2 — UI", 2026-10-25.

## Summary

The product is called **Castle Control**, a working name that is not a cleared trademark. Its UI comes from two contest rounds that the owner decided on 2026-10-25:

- **Round 1.** Two seats built six brands, each with a landing page and all four module pages, on illustrative Overlord-sandbox data: Castle Control, Multiball (twice), Proof, Shepherd and Overprint. The owner chose **Overprint** (B/3) as "the best combination of style, clarity", noted that it fits Shadowrunr well, and rejected its brand.
- **Final round.** Two seats each rebranded Overprint's craft. Overprint's typography, paper background, ink-and-halftone art and app density were kept. One seat made Castle Control and the other made Shepherd. **Castle Control won.**

The app pages therefore come from Overprint's layouts with Castle Control's names, mark, inks and voice. The landing page is a new design: the keep in cross-section, printed in spot inks.

The winning prototype runs from disk on illustrative data. It is evidence for the port, not a code base to copy; the port contract at the end of this file says how it moves into the real app.

## Brand

**The idea.** A streamer should feel sovereign and safe. The channel is a keep, chat is the crowd at the gate, and the four tools are chambers the streamer commands. The brand is *printed, not rendered*: a gig-poster print in three heraldic spot inks plus a key colour, on warm paper.

- **Tagline:** "Your channel is your keep."
- **Mark:** the keep-bubble, a chat bubble with a battlement top, printed in gules with a gate lit in or. One shared component draws it everywhere.
- **Voice:** courteous and brief. Every castle word sits beside its plain function, so a streamer never has to learn the metaphor to use the tool.

| Chamber | Module id | Ink (tincture) | Hex | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lot Chamber | `lottery` | Gules (red) | `#E4472E` | Wax seals and banners |
| Gatehouse | `moderator` | Azure (blue) | `#3F86DC` | The watch, water, the moat |
| Beacons | `visuals` | Or (gold) | `#F2B233` | Fire and gilt |
| Map Room | `studio` | Key (black) | `#16181D` | The map table |

Inks overprint with a multiply blend, so two overlapping inks make a third colour, as on a real press. Text uses darker companions of each ink (`--azure-text` `#1D5BAA`, `--gules-text` `#B3321B`, `--or-text` `#7A5800`) for contrast on paper.

## Type, paper and tokens

The prototype declares every token once, in `tokens.css`. The port extracts these values unchanged: names may change, pixels may not.

| Role | Token | Stack in the prototype |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display (titles, chamber names) | `--font-display` | Bahnschrift, Arial Narrow, Roboto Condensed, Helvetica Neue, Impact |
| Serif (taglines, italic asides) | `--font-serif` | Iowan Old Style, Palatino Linotype, Palatino, Georgia |
| UI text | `--font-ui` | Segoe UI Variable Text, Segoe UI, system-ui, -apple-system, Helvetica Neue, Arial |
| Live figures (tabular) | `--font-num` | ui-monospace, Cascadia Mono, SF Mono, Consolas, Menlo |

- **Paper:** `--paper` `#F4F0E6`, with a grain texture. `--paper-2` `#EAE4D4`, card `#FBF9F3`, rules `#D9D2BE`, key ink `#16181D`.
- **Dark stock:** a full theme under `[data-theme="stock"]`. It sets paper `#121317` and text `#F2EEE3`, lightens the ink-text companions, and switches the blend to screen. The landing and the app share the toggle, and it exists because a four-hour stream needs it.
- **Signals:** ok `#0A9A5E`, warn `#E8A800`, danger `#CC2149`, info `#1D6FC4`. Their dark-stock values are lighter.
- **Shape and motion:** radii of 3, 6 and 12 px; one easing curve, `cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)`.

**Type across platforms.** The stacks lean on Windows faces: Bahnschrift and Segoe UI Variable. To keep the look on macOS and Linux, the app bundles open-licence fallbacks with close metrics after the system faces. The Windows look is unchanged. Chosen by the plan, not by the owner.

## The app shell

Every chamber page shares one frame, built for 1440x900 with OBS open beside it, and still usable at 1280x800.

- **The rampart (masthead), always visible:**
  - the ON AIR stamp with viewers and uptime;
  - Twitch and OBS connection marks;
  - the Gatehouse chip, showing "Armed n | Watching n", or "Bridge up" when Shield Mode is on;
  - a sample-data or simulated label whenever data is not live;
  - settings, the theme toggle and the Steward button.
- **The chambers column:** the four chambers, each with its live figure, such as "3 draws · 87 lots" or "3 held · 2 watching". Below them sit the whole-keep and brand-sheet links, and a "the hall now" footer with viewers, chatters, tier and moat.
- **The page header:** a breadcrumb ("Castle Control › Chamber 2 of 4 › Gatehouse"), the chamber title in display type with its ink ghost, a one-line plain description, and page-level actions.
- **Panels:** numbered panels (2.1, 2.2 …) separated by rules rather than cards. Live figures use tabular numerals, and body text is at least 13 px.
- **Nested pages:** heavy detail opens as its own page with a breadcrumb and Back, never a drawer. Examples are the writ (proof bundle) and the drill (replay).
- **Keymap:** keys 1–4 switch chambers and `/` asks the Steward. The port keeps these as one keymap table, with a conflict check.

Two invariants hold on every page: the streamer can tell whether they are live, and what the Gatehouse is doing (armed or watching).

## Chamber pages

Each chamber page leads with a live hero band that shows what the module is doing right now, followed by dense numbered panels.

**Lot Chamber (lottery manager)**

- **Hero.** The roll: an entry count in display type, the pool's description, and the roll's state (open, lots arriving, held out by cooldown). The lots appear as numbered tickets above a dot field, with dots coloured by cohort (subscribers, new accounts, raiders). Its actions are Seal the roll and Pause intake.
- **1.1 Draws you have:** saved draws, with No prize, Prize and Paid weight blocked tags.
- **1.2 The roll:** look up whether a handle is in the pool.
- **1.3 Next draw setup:** name, content (a question, an idea, a prize or a jury seat), pool, keyword, restriction, a free !join path with equal odds, weights, winners, cooldown and cohort cap.
- **1.4 Fairness you can verify:** the four steps (seal, public round announced, round arrives, cast) plus a test-mode note.
- **1.5 On stream:** the draw's visual shape.
- **Nested:** the writ (proof bundle and recompute).

**Gatehouse (AI moderator)**

- **Hero.** The room, in one row of cells: garrison orders now, the tier ladder with hysteresis (Hearth, Hall, Courtyard, Tourney field), the moat (2, 4 or 6 s), the held count, and Raise the drawbridge.
- **2.2 The Herald's chart:** opinion directions over 15 minutes, filterable by all messages, emotes only, text only or a single emote. Each direction has a figure and a 3-minute delta.
- **2.1 The live room:** every chat line with its Pass, Hold or Bar verdict, filterable and searchable, with a Pause live action.
- **2.3 The room:** tier table and lease table.
- **2.4 At the portcullis:** held messages with Pass and Strike.
- **The rules:** a table of rule, match, action and an armed/watching switch.
- **The gate roll:** the decision ledger as a right-hand column, with Undo and Petition on each entry.
- **The drill:** a past night run through today's rules; the full replay opens as a nested page.

**Beacons (contribution visuals)**

- **Hero.** Three beacon cards (offering altar, gilded camera frame, hype border), each with a halftone preview, its current look, a blaze percentage, a range ruler, its inputs and its decay.
- **3.2 Kindling curve:** an editable curve from input to blaze, with the current point marked, plus decay and gift-sub weighting controls.
- **3.3 Test fire:** a stylised preview, an intensity slider, and "Test fire: 50-sub bomb".
- **The feed:** contribution events, and the OBS browser-source URL to copy.

**Map Room (Extension studio)**

- **Hero.** The map table: a hex map with side filters (house banners, fog), click-to-inspect, the turn clock and Run a test turn.
- **4.1 Pieces:** component toggles (board, turn clock, move menu, side tallies, standing meter).
- **4.2 Orders:** who drives (human, AI streamer or both), what viewers can do, cadence, and sealing.
- **4.3 Templates:** one shell, five maps.
- **4.4 Spyglass:** a desktop and mobile viewer preview.
- **4.5 Test status:** sand table, muster (hosted test with allowlisted accounts), proclaimed.

## The landing

The landing is the project's website, not a screen inside the app. It ships as a static site from the same repo and shares the tokens package.

- **Hero:**
  - a fixed-proportion 1600x1000 sheet, scaled from 1280x800 to 2560x1440;
  - the keep in cross-section: gatehouse, cutaway hall and map room, beacon tower and watch tower, over a moat;
  - the stone shaded in halftone, with crop and registration marks at the corners;
  - a stamped "Castle Control" wordmark and the tagline card.
- **Intro.** Five plates arrive out of register and snap into it: stone, beacon fire, the Lot Chamber, the Gatehouse, then the map. Then the drawbridge drops and control passes to the visitor. It takes under three seconds and can be skipped. Reduced motion gets a calm version.
- **Choosing.** Hovering a chamber ghosts the other plates and prints its name in the sky, in its own ink. Clicking opens a full-bleed print of that chamber, such as the gate roll beside the portcullis, the moat gauge and the Herald's chart. A feature list sits one level deeper, with Back at every level.
- **The banner bar.** A bar always on screen with all four chambers and Open the app.
- **Mobile (390x844).** A stacked wordmark, four chamber strips with their art, and the banner bar.
- **Honesty labels.** Stylised illustration, drawn rather than captured; sample data from a fictional channel; a working name; not affiliated with Twitch.

## Port contract

**Where the prototype lives.** The winning prototype is kept beside this plan, in [`prototype/`](prototype/). Open `prototype/index.html` from disk; it loads its own fallback copy of the sample data. It holds:

- `index.html` with `landing.css` and `landing.js` (the landing);
- `lottery.html`, `moderator.html`, `visuals.html` and `studio.html`, each with an `app-*.js` file;
- `app.css` and `app.js` (the shared shell);
- `tokens.css`, `mark.js` and `brand.html`;
- `NOTES.md`, the seat's own account of the design and of what changed in the final round.

The new repo copies it once into its own `prototype/` as a read-only reference. (It was built in the contest arena at `.contest/arena/toolkit-landing-r2/`, which is not version-controlled.)

**Known defects to fix in the port, not to carry:**

- At desktop sizes the landing wordmark's L feet are hidden, so it reads "CASTIE CONTROI". It renders correctly at 390 px.
- At 1280 px the Beacons and Map Room pages wrap a column below, so they run tall.
- The Lot Chamber hero can ellipsise a long draw name.

**Exit for the UI port.** All four must hold at once (from the contest's port-quality contract):

1. **Fidelity.** Every page is compared with the prototype at 1440x900 and 1280x800, with zero deviations on the named roles: paper, inks, type, panel rules and the rampart. Every named interaction has been driven.
2. **Gates at zero, each proven red once.** This covers:
   - import boundaries and cycles;
   - no `any` and no `as unknown as`;
   - `JSON.parse` only at the contract door;
   - a 400-line file cap;
   - token drift, and no raw colours or px values in component styles;
   - a type floor of 13 px.
3. **Budgets.** Every list that can outgrow one screen is virtualised and proven at ten times the sample's volume, for example 4,200 chat lines instead of 420. Each route has a first-load bundle budget, measured by byte count.
4. **Prototype code stays out of the product.** The port extracts the prototype's design; it does not copy its code. Hand-written CSS moves as co-located stylesheets over generated token variables, not as approximate utility classes.
