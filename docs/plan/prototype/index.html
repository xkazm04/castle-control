Before you open the gates.

Castle Control is a free, open-source desktop app for Twitch streamers. It is the keep, not the show: you bring your banners, your rules and your art, whether you paint, speedrun or do a bit of everything.

Drill before you go live

Every chamber has a preview or a simulation. Nothing runs for the first time on a live audience.

One verdict before everything

A message the Gatehouse barred never counts toward a draw, a vote, a chant or a consensus.

Lots you can check

The Lot Chamber seals the entry list, shows its hash, and takes winners from a future public randomness round (drand). The animation only shows a result already decided. Paid-only entry and paid weight are blocked by default for prize draws.

Armed only when you say so

The Gatehouse starts Watching (shadow mode): it says what it would do and does nothing. Bans can need a human click. Every action carries its reason, with an undo where Twitch allows one.

A steward, if you want one

Ask the Steward in plain words; it drafts a writ, and nothing changes until you seal it. It runs on your own AI CLI, a local model or an API key. Every chamber works without it.

Honest about Twitch

It connects through EventSub, the Helix API, an OBS browser source and one Twitch Extension. The moat (chat delay) buys 2, 4 or 6 seconds, not magic. Events missed in a dropout are not replayed. An Extension needs Twitch’s review (8–14 days) before release.

Chamber 1 of 4 · the Lot Chamber · lottery

Lottery manager

Cast lots anyone can check. The Lot Chamber runs the giveaways, question picks and jury seats you already do in chat, and hands every viewer a writ to verify the result afterwards.

Enter the Lot Chamber →
  1. Seal the roll. The entry list is frozen and its hash shown on stream beside a future public randomness round. Winners come from that round’s value.
  2. A writ anyone can recompute. The proof bundle is downloadable, and the animation only ever shows a result already decided.
  3. Safe by default. Paid-only entry and paid extra weight are blocked for prize draws; a free path with equal odds is one click away. A prize needs a value, an official-rules template and the “Twitch does not sponsor this promotion” line on stream.
  4. Your rules, your shape. Pool, weights, winners, cooldowns, cohort caps, and a drum, wheel, deck or ballot box on stream. sample: 87 lots on the roll

Setting up a draw

  • Pool: all chatters, followers, subscribers, channel-point redeemers, people who typed a keyword.
  • Rules: entry weights, number of winners, cooldowns (“no repeat winners for 3 draws”), cohort caps (“raiders fill at most a third”).
  • Content: a prize, a question, an idea, a seat on a jury.

Fairness and safety

  • Entry list frozen, hash shown on stream, winners from a future public randomness round (a beacon such as drand); proof bundle downloadable.
  • Blocked by default for prize draws: paid-only entry and paid extra weight. Warnings: subscriber-only entry, channel-point entry with no free path, no region and age setting.
  • Required for prizes: a value, an official-rules template, the Twitch disclaimer on stream. Draws with no prize (questions, ideas) run without friction.

Main page

  • The draws you have, the live entry pool as it fills, the next draw’s configuration, a preview of its on-stream visual, and past draws with their writs.

Chamber 2 of 4 · the Gatehouse · moderator

AI moderator

Nobody walks in unseen. The Gatehouse is one moderator that guards the room and reads it. It starts by watching, and only acts when you arm it.

Enter the Gatehouse →
  1. Watching first. Shadow is the default: the Gatehouse says what it would do and does nothing. Armed, it acts; a ban can need a human click.
  2. Every hold has a reason. One gate log of every hold, release, removal and mode change, with its reason, an undo where Twitch allows one, and a path for petitions (appeals).
  3. The Herald’s chart. The strongest directions of opinion in chat right now, filtered by emote or emotion, rising and falling over the last minutes.
  4. Drill on last night. Replay a past stream’s chat through today’s rules before you switch them on. sample: 18,420 messages → 212 held, 96 barred

The gate

  • A non-moderator chat delay of 2, 4 or 6 s (the moat), a queue of messages AutoMod held for review (at the portcullis), and Shield Mode as the emergency switch (raise the drawbridge).

Garrison orders (the room governor)

  • Slow, follower-only, emote-only and unique chat, switched by room-size tiers with hysteresis (no flapping) and timed leases (a mode ends on its own).

Rules on people

  • Streamer-set rules (words, links, spam, repeats, new accounts) map to warn, delete, timeout or ban. An optional LLM classifier handles what rules cannot express.
  • Every action has a reason, an undo where Twitch allows one, and a path for appeals. One verdict before everything: barred messages never count.

Main page

  • The live room, the Herald’s chart, the latest gate log, Watching or Armed state, and the entry to the drill (the replay simulator).

Chamber 3 of 4 · the Beacons · visuals

Contribution visuals

Gifts light fires on your stream. Contributions change how it looks, never what it decides. Map bits, subs, gift subs and Hype Train to a blaze, and let it die down.

Climb to the Beacons →
  1. Kindle by the numbers. Glow, size, particles, colour or sound over ranges you set, dying down over time. sample: halves every 5 min
  2. Frames, borders, objects. A frame around the camera, a screen border, or an altar, bonfire, crystal or banner: HTML, CSS and JS layers you design.
  3. Test fire before live. A live preview with an intensity slider and a “simulate a 50-sub gift bomb” button.
  4. Paste the feed into OBS. Each beacon is served as an OBS browser source URL, and optionally into the Extension overlay.

Inputs and outputs

  • Inputs: Bits, subs, gift subs, Hype Train level, channel-point redemptions, charity donations, goals.
  • Outputs: a frame around the camera, a screen border, or an on-screen object such as an altar, a bonfire, a crystal or a banner, mapped to intensity ranges (glow, size, particles, colour, sound) with decay.

Asset designer

  • Assets are HTML, CSS and JS layers. The Steward can generate artwork through an image-generation API with your own key. Live preview with an intensity slider.

Delivery and main page

  • Served as an OBS browser source (copy the URL into OBS), optionally into the Extension overlay.
  • Main page: the lit beacons and their blaze, the event feed driving them, the kindling curve editor and the preview.

Chamber 4 of 4 · the Map Room · studio

Extension studio

Draw what viewers touch, without writing code. One reviewed Twitch Extension, dressed with your own data and art.

Enter the Map Room →
  1. One Extension, edited as data. Layout, components, viewer controls and art are data. The studio never edits code, so a new design needs no new review.
  2. Five templates. A hex map where viewers pick moves, a choice board, a viewer card, the draw, a contribution meter.
  3. Who drives, what viewers do. The human streamer, an AI streamer or both; vote, pick a tile or join a side, with cadence and limits.
  4. Sand table, muster, proclaimed. Run it locally, then on your own channel for allowlisted test accounts; Twitch review takes 8–14 days before release. sample: hosted test, 12 accounts

One Extension

  • The app ships one reviewed, data-driven Twitch Extension. The studio edits layout, components, viewer controls and art.

Templates and settings

  • Hex map, choice board, viewer card, the lottery’s draw, a contribution meter.
  • Operation: who drives it and what viewers can do, with cadence and limits. Testing: local test, hosted test (allowlisted accounts), released.

Main page

  • The layout canvas, the component list, the viewer-side preview (desktop and mobile), the operator controls and the test status.
