# Preview and Proclaim

## Philosophy
A streamer cares about what the audience sees, so the Steward edits the show itself in a preview, and the streamer proclaims it to air. The first screen is a 16:9 preview of the stream: Malakor's crypt, two doors and the camera. Every chamber is a layer on that picture: the urn and writ, the chat overlay and vote bar, the altar and frames, and the war-table panel.

A small Program monitor shows what is on air. The red Proclaim button is the human seal.

## How it stays readable at scale
- One picture holds four layers. Below it, one strip per chamber has On air and Draft lamps.
- Drafts carry a dashed DRAFT frame; held chat shows greyed and never reaches Program.
- Clicking a layer (or pressing 1–4) opens its inspector:
  - the writ, with the fairness box and past writs;
  - holds, rules, the moat, the drill and consensus;
  - the visuals' ranges and the 60 offerings;
  - the hex map and its test ladder.

## The wow moment
Say "put a question draw on screen". The Steward's lantern glides across the preview and the urn appears as a draft. "Make it 5 winners" re-inks the overlay. Holding Proclaim seals the roll, with a real SHA-256, and puts it in Program. When you cast, the winners appear in the preview first and reach Program only when you proclaim them.

## Known limits
- Dropped on purpose: the companion as a character, and any page per chamber.
- The game scene is stylised sample art. Nothing reaches a stream.
- The Steward, the public round and "Hold to speak" are simulated.
- Map tiles are generated and gift weighting is illustrative.
